What the participant sees

Read the motion

A pelvis camera supplies 128 × 96 RGB and 16-bit depth in millimetres. The reference measures the moving edge of each amber plate across frames and estimates whether enough time remains to cross. Joint angles and velocities, body motion, foot contacts and the decoder history arrive with each decision. There is no door clock, service-side flag or room camera on the participant wire.

Send a body command

The public reference reads the offset and height of the shelf from the camera. It encodes a ten-pose gait into a row of 64 integer FSQ codes in [0,31]. The frozen SONIC V1 decoder sets 29 body targets every 20 ms. Native physics runs ten 2 ms intervals per row. A wrist, finger or foot hitting the shutter or furniture can end the course.

A controlled test against each powered leaf made only a distal finger touch. Both terminated on the first 2 ms interval, with the pelvis still 0.306 m before the door.

What makes a route count

A working shutter is commanded open over 1.0 s, held open for 4.2 s, closed over 1.0 s, then held closed for 5.8 s. The two phases vary independently. The judge checks the real opening at the plate and again at the cabinet plane, then any fixed approach gate. After the turn, the head must enter the selected rack under its solid shelf and the whole body must clear the back. It also checks the blue stop, a light-signalled pivot and the green stop with the same uninterrupted robot and decoder history.

Each official draw has 16 courses: eight with two fully serviceable shutters, four left-only and four right-only. Eight have a stationary approach gate. Twelve final racks are low and four high, with eight offsets on each side. The robot must leave the rack with its whole body and re-stand with the physical top of its head at least 1.25 m high throughout a 0.75 s final dwell. The horizon is 4,350 rows (87 s).

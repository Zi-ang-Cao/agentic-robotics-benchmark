What the controller receives

From pixels to 29 joints

Every decision receives a 128 × 96 pelvis RGB image, aligned depth in millimetres, joint angles and velocities, body motion, foot contacts and decoder history. A row of 64 integers from 0 to 31 drives the frozen SONIC V1 decoder for 20 ms. The reference chooses one row at a time; the hands stay open.

Three stops, one continuous state

Cross one or two real gaps in order. Stop before the blue beacon, hold for another 1.50 s, pivot toward the heading signal, then walk and stop before green. Each stop requires 0.75 s of stable posture. The participant receives the new cue through its camera. Native contact checks run every 2 ms.

Formal benchmark results

Each 16-course draw includes four single openings, six pairs on the same side and six pairs on opposite sides. A strict completion requires every crossing, all three stops and zero cabinet contacts.

The first reference fell short in one of two ALE calibration draws (14/16); the film uses the subsequent controller e0674ac3. In a separately registered native qualification, the first reference finished 16/16 courses, made 48 stops and crossed all 29 openings with zero cabinet contacts.

What counts as a collision

MuJoCo checks contact for the whole robot, including open fingers, arms, hips and feet. Deliberately placing the wrist alone and the ankle alone against a cabinet ended both tests at the first 2 ms interval. Across 24 wall-contact fixtures, all 24 terminated; 12 positions centered in clear gaps registered zero cabinet contacts.
